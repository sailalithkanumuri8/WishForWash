The Issue

1 in 4 people still can't count on safe water.

It's easy to picture the water crisis as someone else's problem. The newest global count says otherwise: billions of people can't rely on the tap, toilet or sink most of us never think about.

  • 2.1 billion

    people lack safely managed drinking water

  • 3.4 billion

    lack safely managed sanitation

  • 1.7 billion

    lack basic hygiene services at home

Source: WHO/UNICEF Joint Monitoring Programme, 2025 update

Data note

Some of our earlier sources cited 26% and 46%. We replaced those older figures with the 2025 JMP update, so every number on this site is current.

What is WASH?

WASH stands for water, sanitation and hygiene. Health experts group them because each one only works when the others do.

  • WA

    Water

    Safe drinking water at home, there when you need it and free from contamination.

  • S

    Sanitation

    A toilet that keeps waste away from people, plus a system that safely treats it.

  • H

    Hygiene

    A place at home to wash your hands with soap and water.

Why it happens

Our research points to four forces that keep safe water out of reach:

  • Population growth

    More people share the same rivers and aquifers, and cities must treat more wastewater every year.

  • Aging infrastructure

    Pipes and treatment plants wear out. When an old main breaks, safe water stops flowing.

  • Limited funding

    Water systems are expensive to build and maintain, so repairs often wait until something fails.

  • Weak water management

    Without shared data and planning, water gets overused, polluted or fought over.

6

UN Sustainable Development Goal 6

Clean water and sanitation for all by 2030

SDG 6 asks every country to ensure the availability and sustainable management of water and sanitation for all.

  • 6.1 Drinking water
  • 6.2 Sanitation and hygiene
  • 6.3 Water quality
  • 6.4 Water efficiency

Water ripples into other goals:

  • SDG 3 Good health Safe water and toilets prevent disease.
  • SDG 11 Sustainable cities Growing cities need pipes and plants that last.
  • SDG 14 Life below water What washes off our streets flows to the sea.

Solutions

Every one of these problems has a fix someone is already building.

Flip each card to see the fix and the proof behind it. None of these needs a miracle, just good design and people willing to show up.

  • The problem

    Not enough clean water to go around

    The fix: wastewater recycling

    Treated wastewater can become a new, reliable water source for farms, industry and even drinking water.

    Proof: a peer-reviewed review in npj Clean Water.

    Source: npj Clean Water (2020)

  • The problem

    Stormwater rushing polluted runoff into creeks

    The fix: green infrastructure

    Bioswales, rain gardens and permeable surfaces soak up stormwater before it hits the creek.

    Proof: EPA expects planned green infrastructure in the Proctor Creek watershed to cut runoff and improve water quality.

    Source: US EPA Region 4

  • The problem

    Nobody knows what's in the water

    The fix: community monitoring

    Chattahoochee Riverkeeper trains volunteers to sample urban streams for bacteria.

    Proof: EPA placed its Proctor Creek study sites at those long-running volunteer stations so the data could be compared.

    Source: US EPA Region 4

  • The problem

    Drought-year irrigation drains rivers

    The fix: smarter irrigation

    A $49.8 million grant is moving drought-year irrigation in southwest Georgia to about 242 deep aquifer wells, with aquifer and stream monitoring.

    Proof: deeper wells take pressure off the streams farms and wildlife share.

    Source: Albany State University

  • The problem

    Sanitation that doesn't fit the community

    The fix: affordable sanitation design

    Georgia Tech students design low-cost sanitation around what a community actually needs.

    Proof: EWB-GT's Malawi "Sanitation Solutions" project placed third at the 2024 GAWP student design competition.

    Source: GT Civil and Environmental Engineering

What we do

  • Research

    Using human-centered research to better understand water, sanitation, and hygiene challenges, and the people most affected by them.

  • Design & Innovation

    Creating practical, affordable sanitation products and systems that are built around real community needs.

  • Education & Advocacy

    Making WASH more approachable through workshops, storytelling, and programs that bring more diverse voices into the field.

Georgia Tech

Georgia Tech is already a living water lab.

Sustainability Next, Georgia Tech's 10-year sustainability roadmap, calls on GT to be a global sustainability thought leader and to catalyze innovation through education and research. Wish for Wash is students doing exactly that.

  • EcoCommons

    80 acres

    of planned green space designed to cut campus stormwater runoff by 50%.

  • Kendeda Building

    Rain in, clean water out

    The building harvests rain and treats its wastewater on site.

  • Campus cisterns

    Rain, reused

    Cisterns across campus catch rainwater so it can be reused instead of running off.

  • Stamps Field

    44,500 cubic feet

    of water that infiltration cells under the field can release back into the ground.
